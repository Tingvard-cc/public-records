Tingvard does not agree with the conclusion that this Info governance action is unconstitutional.

The rationale presented conflates social influence with constitutional effect. Under the Constitution, an Info action has no binding authority. It cannot impose requirements, restrict participation, or create conditions for proposal eligibility. As such, claims that the framework introduces barriers to participation under Article II, §1 are not supported.

The argument instead relies on how DReps may choose to behave. However, Article II, §4 explicitly vests evaluative authority in DReps. Their choice to follow a framework, prefer structured submissions, or reference prior Info actions is an expression of that authority, not a violation of it.

Similarly, references to Tenet 9 are overstretched. The Constitution does not require DReps to evaluate all proposals identically or without strategic preference. It requires fairness in access and process, both of which remain intact.

Tingvard acknowledges that Info actions can shape coordination and influence governance norms. However, this is a feature of an open governance system, not a constitutional defect.

Accordingly, Tingvard finds no constitutional basis to deem this governance action unconstitutional.
