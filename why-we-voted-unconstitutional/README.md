## Why we voted Unconstitutional

At first glance, the proposal for Cardano Summit 2026 and TOKEN2049 Singapore looks solid. It clearly explains what the funds are for, how they’ll be used, who is responsible, and how everything will be tracked. It checks all the boxes required by the Cardano Constitution when it comes to structure and transparency.

So why did we vote Unconstitutional?

Because being well-structured is not enough.

---


It’s not just about following the rules

The Constitution doesn’t only require proposals to be complete and well-documented. It also includes guiding principles—called Tenets—that ensure resources are used responsibly.

One of these is simple:

> Don’t spend resources unreasonably.




---

The key issue: a big increase without a clear explanation

The largest part of the request is for the Cardano Summit. The cost has increased significantly—more than doubling.

As part of the approved 2025 Summit treasury withdrawal, a 2025–2029 plan was presented, estimating the 2026 Summit at around USD 1.2M.

In this proposal, the request is USD 2.5M.

Higher costs aren’t automatically a problem. But:

👉 The proposal doesn’t clearly explain why the cost increased this much.

It describes a new strategy, but doesn’t clearly connect it to the price.


---

Why that matters

This isn’t about whether the event is “worth it.” That’s for DReps.

Our role is to ask:

> Is there enough information to show this is a reasonable use of treasury funds?



In this case, the answer is no.


---

The takeaway

> If you ask for more than what was previously planned and approved, you need to clearly show why.



That clarity wasn’t there—and that’s why we voted Unconstitutional.
