# Critical roles cannot run on goodwill forever

Cardano governance is permissionless.

That is a strength. Anyone can participate. Anyone can propose. Anyone can build. Anyone can challenge the direction of the system and ask the community to choose a different path.

But permissionless does not mean consequence-free. It does not mean governance happens by magic. It does not mean the ledger can keep moving without people taking responsibility for the decisions that allow it to move.

For every governance action, DReps must review, assess, and vote. For constitutional matters, the Constitutional Committee must assess whether the action is constitutional. From time to time, SPOs are also placed in a position where their participation is required for governance to function as intended.

These are not symbolic roles. They are critical functions of the ledger.

And right now, too much of that work is being carried by people who are expected to show up, read, assess, vote, justify, explain, defend, absorb pressure, and take public criticism for free.

That is not sustainable.

## Voluntary does not mean valueless

The Constitutional Committee is voluntary. Being a DRep is voluntary. No one is forced to take on these roles, and that should remain true. Cardano should not become a system where participation in governance is closed off or professionalized beyond reach.

But voluntary does not mean valueless.

People step into these roles because they want to contribute to a critical function of the Cardano ledger. They do it because they believe the system matters. They do it because they are willing to take responsibility where responsibility is needed.

The question is whether the ecosystem respects that contribution enough to make it sustainable.

At the moment, the answer is not good enough.

## The pressure is already visible

We are seeing growing pressure on Constitutional Committee members. We are seeing the same pressure on DReps. Expectations keep increasing. The amount of work keeps increasing. The public scrutiny keeps increasing.

But the compensation remains zero.

We are already seeing the effect of this in the Constitutional Committee election. Registration has been open for a month, and so far only four candidates have registered. That is the bare minimum needed to fill the four seats that will be vacated in September when the terms of four CC members expire.

That should worry everyone.

This is not a healthy signal from a system that depends on capable people stepping forward to carry one of its most critical governance functions.

Likewise, we are seeing a slow and steady decline in active DReps. Some have retired. Others are still registered, but no longer active in any meaningful sense. The reason is not hard to understand. The work is real, the responsibility is real, and the cost in time and attention is real.

This creates a very obvious problem. The people who ensure that others can continue building, proposing, receiving funding, and executing work are themselves expected to carry the governance burden without support.

Do we really want to continue down that road?

Tingvard’s answer is no.

## Our position

To be clear, Tingvard stood for election while openly supporting governance incentives. We encouraged compensation for critical governance roles. We did not demand it as a condition for doing the work.

Tingvard is not going anywhere during the term we were elected for. We will continue to assess actions. We will continue to vote. We will continue to take the responsibility we accepted.

But we ask the community, the founding entities, and vendors to respect the six-epoch period the Constitutional Committee is given to submit votes. This work takes time. It requires reading, discussion, judgement, and coordination. It should not be treated as an inconvenience because someone else wants faster execution.

We will also be clear about something else: Tingvard will not stand for reelection unless compensation is handled before our term ends.

## This is not unique to us

This is not unique to us.

Atlantic Council has already decided to retire due to lack of compensation. Others have chosen not to stand for reelection for the same reason. We are losing institutional knowledge because we refuse to pay for the roles that preserve it.

The same is happening among DReps. Several have retired because the effort required is not worth carrying indefinitely without compensation.

That is not a failure of those people. That is a system design problem.

## Seven months of silence

At the Cardano Summit in November, attending Constitutional Committee members and several DReps were invited to a meeting by Murasaki from EMURGO to discuss this exact issue. At the end of that meeting, it was communicated that this was only the start, and that the work would continue.

Seven months later, we have heard nothing from EMURGO about continuing that work.

That silence matters.

Cardano governance cannot mature if critical contributors are treated as infinitely renewable volunteer labor. It cannot ask people to carry legal, procedural, constitutional, and reputational responsibility while pretending that appreciation is enough.

Appreciation does not pay for time.

And time is exactly what governance requires.

If Cardano wants serious governance, it needs to treat governance seriously. That means compensating the roles that are necessary for the system to function.

Not as a handout.

Not as a privilege.

As payment for critical work.
