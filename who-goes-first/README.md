# The Constitution Never Told Us Who Goes First

## Background

The current Treasury Withdrawal framework creates a structural coordination problem as the active Net Change Limit (NCL) approaches exhaustion.

Tingvard considers it important to be transparent about this reality before the ecosystem reaches a situation where concurrent governance actions compete for remaining NCL capacity.

The Constitution requires that Treasury Withdrawals must not exceed the active NCL under Article II, Section 7, §3 and Appendix I, TREASURY-02a. At the same time, the current governance process provides no mechanism to dynamically recalculate available NCL capacity during overlapping voting periods.

---

## Structural Coordination Problem

This creates edge cases where:

- ordering and timing may materially affect outcomes,
- DRep preferences may not be fully reflected in practice,
- and proposals may become constitutionally problematic despite uncertainty around which proposals will ultimately pass DRep thresholds.

---

## Tingvard Approach

As the active NCL approaches exhaustion, Tingvard will generally prioritize Treasury Withdrawals based on submission order. Earlier submitted proposals will generally be assessed first against the remaining available NCL capacity.

If a proposal would exceed the remaining NCL at the time of assessment, it will generally be judged unconstitutional. A later proposal that remains within the remaining limit may still be constitutional.

---

## Potential Edge Cases

Tingvard recognizes that this approach may produce unwanted outcomes in certain edge cases. A proposal judged unconstitutional due to NCL exhaustion may later have fit within the limit if earlier proposals fail DRep thresholds, expire, or are rejected. Likewise, submission timing may in practice become more important than broader ecosystem preference or proposal merit.

The current framework may therefore unintentionally create:

- first-come-first-served dynamics,
- reduced responsiveness to emerging ecosystem needs,
- barriers for later proposals regardless of community support,
- and potential attack vectors through saturation of the active NCL.

---

## Structural Governance Issue

This is ultimately a structural issue with the current governance framework rather than necessarily with individual proposals themselves.

Tingvard therefore believes the ecosystem should seriously consider improvements to treasury coordination mechanisms, budget sequencing, or constitutional amendments clarifying how NCL assessment should function during concurrent voting periods.

Until such clarifications exist, Tingvard will continue to apply constitutional judgment in good faith based on the Constitution currently in force, while also exercising reasonable constitutional discretion where necessary to preserve the broader intent and integrity of the governance system.
